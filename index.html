Network Epistemology Testbed

Interactive implementations of models from network epistemology: how the structure of who-talks-to-whom in a scientific community shapes what that community comes to believe. Every model runs in the browser with adjustable parameters, seeded runs you can replay, and a Monte Carlo runner for measuring effects.

Models

  • Model 1 · Two-armed bandit The Zollman Effect

    Bayesian agents choose between a known action and an uncertain, slightly better one, sharing results with network neighbours. Sparse networks can be more reliable than complete ones, but only when the problem is hard. After Bala & Goyal (1998), Zollman (2007) and Rosenstock, Bruner & O’Connor (2017).

  • Model 2 · Polarization Scientific Polarization

    The same bandit, but scientists trust evidence less the more its source disagrees with them, and update by Jeffrey’s rule. A second absorbing state appears: believers keep producing evidence that the doubters no longer accept. After O’Connor & Weatherall (2018).

  • Analysis · Model 1 with exploration What Is a Cycle Worth?

    Prices network structure in exploration. How many exploratory tests per agent does a complete network need to be as reliable as a cycle, a star or a grid? The answer works as an exchange rate, and very different networks turn out to be worth the same. Includes Kummerfeld & Zollman’s (2016) model, where exploration has a price.

  • Model 3 · Beta beliefs Transient Diversity

    Neither method’s success rate is known; beliefs are beta distributions, and prior strength measures dogmatism. Sparse networks and stubborn priors are two ways to keep a community from settling too soon, and too much of either stops it settling at all. After Zollman (2010).

  • Model 4 · Conformity Conformity in Science

    Scientists add a payoff for doing what their neighbours did. Beliefs stay honest but actions conform, so people can know the better action and not take it; tight cliques polarize. After Weatherall & O’Connor (2021).

  • Model 5 · Propaganda The Tobacco Strategy

    A propagandist misleads policy makers using only real results, by forwarding the ones that favour the worse action or by funding many small studies. Also: “fair” journalists who slow the public down. After Weatherall, O’Connor & Bruner (2020).

  • Model 6 · Choose your exploration Free-Riding on Exploration

    Each scientist picks their own exploration rate. Leaving the exploring to others pays, so communities explore less than would be best for them, landing where sparse networks help. After Kummerfeld & Zollman (2016).

  • Model 7 · Self-assembling networks Learning Whom to Trust

    No network is given: agents learn by reinforcement whether to look at the world or whom to ask, and the unreliable come to defer to the reliable. Vigilance, crediting only sources that were right, makes big groups smart rather than confused. After Barrett, Skyrms & Mohseni (2019) and Pozzi (2025).

  • Model 8 · Biased agents Intransigently Biased Agents

    A drug company inside the network keeps reporting subtly inflated results and never updates. In a static network the doctors can never settle on the better drug, and more links help. Doctors who learn whom to trust, by testing how plausible each source’s data are, shut it out. After Holman & Bruner (2015).
